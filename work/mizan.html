The problem

Across the UAE, the price you're quoted for everyday services — AC cleaning, a haircut, movers, a gym — often isn't what people actually pay. Markups and opaque pricing leave residents with no reference point, and no way to tell whether a quote is fair or steep.

How it works

Mizan — ميزان, "the scale" — shows two layers side by side: the market estimate (advertised list prices harvested from the web) and the paid range (what residents report actually paying). Pick a service and your area, enter the price you were quoted, and it returns a verdict: fair, steep, or walk away.

The paid layer is community-reported and anonymous, with outlier trimming so one odd price can't skew the result — and it sharpens with every report. Free to use, no paid placements, across all seven emirates.

Design decisions

Two layers, never blended. The obvious version averaged advertised and paid prices into one number. Rejected — the gap between what's advertised and what residents actually pay is the product. Collapsing it hides the exact markup story people come to see.

A verdict, not a dataset. Early screens showed the raw price ranges and stopped. That pushes the judgment back onto the user — the very work Mizan exists to remove. So every check ends in plain language: fair, steep, or walk away.

Trust before scale. Open community averages are easy to skew, so reports are anonymous, per-area, and outlier-trimmed — one odd price can't move a verdict. And it launches deep in fewer service-area cells rather than shallow everywhere, because a wrong answer costs more trust than no answer.

User flow

Three inputs to a verdict — the whole point is that it ends in a judgement, not a chart. A second loop feeds the paid layer: anyone who just checked a price is the best-placed person to report one.

Primary flow — check a price
01Service"What are you paying for?" — 200+ services.
→
02Area"In which area?" — all 7 emirates.
→
03Quote"The price you were quoted" + unit.
→
04VerdictFair, steep, or walk away — against both layers.
Secondary loop — contribute (what keeps the data alive)
01Report paidAnonymous — service, area, what you actually paid.
→
02Trim outliersOne odd price can't move a verdict.
→
03Sharpen rangePaid layer tightens with every report.

States & edge cases

A price tool is only trusted if it admits what it doesn't know. The hardest state isn't a steep quote — it's thin data, where saying nothing beats guessing.

FairQuote sits inside the paid range. Confirm and move on.
SteepAbove paid, below advertised — shows the gap in dirhams.
Walk awayFar above what residents report paying.
Thin dataToo few reports for that service-area cell — says so instead of inventing a range.
Advertised onlyList prices exist, no paid reports yet — one layer shown, labelled.
Outlier caughtA report far outside the spread is trimmed, not published.

Outcome

Shipped. The whole loop — the concept, the two-layer data model, the full interface — built and live as a product residents check before they pay, including an Arabic version.
