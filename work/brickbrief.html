BrickBrief

Run your whole Dubai business on official data. Market reports on the agent's own letterhead, then the client book, daily follow-ups and team tools that keep the next deal coming.

The problem

When a client asks "is this a fair price?", most agents answer with a portal screenshot. It's unbranded, unverifiable, and it makes the agent look like a middleman instead of the expert. The official data exists, but not in a form anyone would put their name on.

The approach

BrickBrief turns Dubai Land Department open data into a document: median prices, price per square metre, sales volume, rents and yields for any of 279 areas, 112 communities and 5,900+ buildings, from registered sales and Ejari rents pulled every week.

The report carries the agent's letterhead: name, agency, brand colour, logo and RERA number. Share it as a link that previews in WhatsApp as a card with the agent's name on it, or save it as a PDF. The agent gets an email the moment the client opens it.

Beyond the report

The report wins the first conversation. The rest of BrickBrief keeps the next ones coming. A client book holds each client's properties, imported from a spreadsheet, with off-plan payment plans and a portfolio page in English or Arabic.

Today is a list each morning of who to contact and why: a lease ending, a handover, an instalment due, a price or rent move. Every message comes drafted. Building watch shows how many leases end in the buildings an agent farms. Agency puts one letterhead across the whole office, with each agent's activity beside it.

Design decisions

A document, not a dashboard. White paper on a dark shell, serif headings, numbered sections, a methodology block and a reference number. The deliverable is something a client is meant to trust, print and keep, so it borrows the anatomy of a real report, not an analytics tool.

Honest numbers over impressive ones. Price movement is measured on transaction-weighted AED per sqm, not raw totals that swing with what happened to sell. Small samples are flagged instead of hidden, and implausible swings are muted with an explanation.

The agent is the hero. BrickBrief's own brand disappears from the deliverable. Recipient links open on the agent's letterhead, and the product's job is to make the sender look rigorous.

Nothing sends without the agent. Today drafts every message, but each one goes from the agent's own WhatsApp when they press Send. Reports never show unit numbers or who owns a unit, and Building watch shows counts only.

Outcome

Shipped. Live at brickbrief.net: area, community and building reports from DLD sales and Ejari rents, refreshed every week, with WhatsApp preview cards, save-to-PDF, read alerts and Arabic area names. Around them sit the client book, Today, Building watch and agency letterheads. The whole pipeline is mine: ETL, aggregation, report system and frontend.

Status. Free early access, onboarding Dubai agents.
