The problem

When a client asks "is this a fair price?", most agents answer with a portal screenshot — unbranded, unverifiable, and it makes the agent look like a middleman instead of the expert. The official data exists, but not in a form anyone would put their name on.

The approach

BrickBrief turns Dubai Land Department open data into a document — median prices, price per square metre, sales volume, rents and yields for any of 279 areas and 5,900+ buildings, aggregated from 1.1M+ recorded transactions.

The report carries the agent's letterhead — name, agency, brand colour, logo and RERA number. Share it as a link or save it as a PDF; a shared link opens on the sender's letterhead, not BrickBrief's.

Design decisions

A document, not a dashboard. White paper on a dark shell, serif headings, numbered sections, a methodology block and a reference number. The deliverable is something a client is meant to trust, print and keep — so it borrows the anatomy of a real report, not an analytics tool.

Honest numbers over impressive ones. Price movement is measured on transaction-weighted AED per sqm — not raw totals that swing with what happened to sell. Small samples are flagged instead of hidden, and implausible swings are muted with an explanation.

The agent is the hero. BrickBrief's own brand disappears from the deliverable. Recipient links open on the agent's letterhead, and the product's job is to make the sender look rigorous.

Outcome

Shipped. Live at brickbrief.net — area and building reports refreshed monthly from DLD open data, with save-to-PDF, tailored sharing and bilingual area names. The whole pipeline is mine: ETL, aggregation, report system and frontend.

Status. Free early access, onboarding Dubai agents.
